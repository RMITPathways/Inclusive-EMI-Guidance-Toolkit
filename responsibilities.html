Working together to reduce barriers

Inclusive EMI depends on clear teaching, students’ engagement and institutional provision. Each group has a role in reducing avoidable language-related barriers while maintaining disciplinary outcomes and standards.

Students should not have to carry the responsibility for overcoming language-related barriers alone. Educators need time, resources and specialist support to fulfil their role.

EMI involves teaching practice as well as English-language proficiency. A difficulty may relate to language, subject knowledge, unfamiliar academic practices, unclear teaching or inaccessible resources. Explore the cause before deciding on a response.

This guidance complements existing institutional procedures.

Who is responsible for what?

Educators

Provide clear, inclusive teaching and assessment.

  • Explain task requirements, disciplinary expectations and assessment criteria.
  • Provide opportunities to prepare, participate, practise and check understanding.
  • Review avoidable barriers in teaching and materials, drawing on students’ perspectives.
  • Contribute observations when further support may be needed, without making unsupported language diagnoses.
  • Continue appropriate teaching support when a student is referred to another service.

Students

Take an active part in learning and agreed support.

  • Prepare for learning and engage with tasks, feedback and opportunities to practise.
  • Ask for clarification and communicate difficulties where possible, using available private routes when preferred.
  • Use multilingual and digital resources within course and assessment rules.
  • Participate in agreed support and explain when it is inaccessible or does not meet the need.
  • Respect others’ contributions and language backgrounds.

Students are not expected to diagnose their own language needs or act as interpreters for classmates.

Institutions

Establish and resource the conditions for inclusive EMI.

  • Plan language demands, teaching and support within program design and review.
  • Coordinate identification of support needs and provide access to appropriately qualified specialists.
  • Provide staff development, collaboration time and accessible learning resources.
  • Establish clear support pathways, referral responsibilities and follow-up arrangements.
  • Address gaps in provision and review implementation, access and outcomes through existing processes.

Program leaders, language specialists and relevant services fulfil different parts of these responsibilities.

Recognising students’ languages

Ethnic-minority students bring varied language repertoires and learning experiences. Vietnamese may be useful, but it should not be assumed to be every student’s first or strongest language.

Where appropriate to the learning purpose and course rules, allow students to draw on languages they know to prepare ideas, explore concepts and clarify understanding. Ensure people who do not share a language can participate, and arrange suitable checks of meaning where needed. Make explicit when English-language performance is itself being assessed.

Choosing the next step

  1. Explore the difficulty with the student.

    Identify the task and what is difficult. Consider teaching clarity, subject understanding, language demands, academic practices and resource access. Avoid assumptions based on ethnicity, accent, silence or entry scores.

  2. Address barriers within the course.

    Clarify instructions, explain expectations, improve access or provide relevant practice. Use student input and examples of work to check whether the response helps.

  3. Arrange additional support when needed.

    Involve academic skills, language or other specialists where the need exceeds classroom support or requires further assessment. Additional support can begin alongside classroom adaptations; students do not need to wait for those adaptations to fail.

  4. Confirm ownership and follow up.

    Agree who will explain the next step and check whether the student reached suitable provision. Follow institutional requirements for consent and information sharing. Escalate unavailable or inaccessible support to the responsible lead.

When classroom support is not enough

Persistent difficulties understanding or producing English across tasks may require specialist language assessment and sustained English-language provision. Difficulty with a particular academic task may require targeted academic skills support. Some students will need both.

Inaccessible platforms, unavailable materials, insufficient support capacity or recurring barriers across courses require institutional action. Repeated referrals or optional self-study resources alone may not address these needs.

A referral adds support; it does not transfer all responsibility away from the educator or resolve a gap in institutional provision.
